Wolfgang Grope was born on 25 March 1932 in Flensburg, Germany. A childhood between Hannover and Dresden, marked by war and the destruction of that city, eventually brought him back north.

He trained as a carpenter, studied interior design, and opened his own architecture practice. Later he became a teacher, instructing art and technical subjects at schools in Husum and Flensburg, and held a teaching position at the University of Kiel. Married twice, father of eight. A man who built houses — for others and for his family.

His own art happened in the basement. In every house he lived in, he made himself a workspace where no one would disturb him. Oil paintings, etchings, drawings. The ceramics he fired in the school kiln when the schedule allowed. A self-taught artist who spent fifty years trying things out — not for the market, not for exhibitions, but because he couldn't help it.

His final ten years he spent in the countryside near Schleswig, with a partner he met while building stoves. He died on 2 January 2013.
